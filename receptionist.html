Questions05 / 05
How does it know I missed the call?

Your phone forwards unanswered calls to a number we set up on your account. The moment a call lands there unanswered, the caller gets the first text. Calls you answer never touch it.

Will customers know it is not a person?

Yes. Its first message says it is the automated assistant for your business. Asked if it is a real person, it says it is not, and that you, a real person, will text back. That is one of 17 scripted customers we test it against. The others include a gas smell, a price hunter, an angry customer, a Polish speaker and someone fishing for a discount code.

What will it not do?

It does not answer the call with a voice. It texts back. It never quotes prices, never books a slot, never promises an arrival time and never takes payment. If a customer asks something it has not been told about your business, it says it will pass that to you and moves on.

What happens if someone smells gas?

It gives the safety line first, before any questions. A gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm gets the gas emergency line, in the UK 0800 111 999, and 999 if anyone is unwell. Sparking, or water reaching lights or sockets, gets a different line: keep away, fuse box only if it is safe, 999 for smoke or fire. Then it takes name, mobile and address and hands the enquiry to you marked emergency. You give us the lines for your area when we set it up.

Where do my customers' details go?

To a model on a server we run ourselves, not a cloud AI like ChatGPT. Nothing is used to train anything. The enquiry goes to your phone as a text, and a copy is kept on our server. The customer is told you will text back.

What happens after delivery?

It runs on our server for $149 a month. Your name, hours, services, area and emergency lines sit in one file, so new hours or a new service is a text to us, not a rebuild. The Twilio or Telnyx number stays on your own account, about $1 to $2 a month plus a few pence per text.
